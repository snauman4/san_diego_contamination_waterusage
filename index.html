    Part 2

    What's in our creeks and lakes?

    Scientists use a simple measurement called conductivity as an early warning for salts and runoff building up in water. Pure water barely carries electricity, but dissolved salts do. Drag the slider to see the idea.

    500
    µS/cm · microsiemens per centimeter
    Does a high reading mean the water is "contaminated"?

    Not necessarily. Much inland San Diego water is naturally salty because of local geology, and imported Colorado River water carries dissolved salts. Tidal lagoons read high naturally. But when a freshwater creek reads in the thousands, salts from irrigation runoff, wastewater, or urban runoff are usually adding up. Conductivity is a flag, not a verdict. It tells us where to look closer. It does not measure bacteria, nutrients or toxic chemicals.

      Bacteria: beaches, creeks & storm drains

      Conductivity can't tell you whether water is safe to swim in. Fecal indicator bacteria can. California posts a beach when a single enterococcus sample tops 104 per 100 mL. Each dot shows the share of 2020–2026 samples above that line. Enterococcus is a warning sign of fecal contamination (from sewage, pets, wildlife), not a direct count of disease-causing germs.

        About the data

        Water use: State Water Board, Urban Retail Water Supplier – Conservation, Supply, and Demand, most recent 12 reported months for 21 San Diego County suppliers. Boundaries: State Water Board System Area Boundary Layer (a 2019 snapshot that is still being verified). Residential gal/person/day = reported residential demand ÷ (service population × days). "Total supplied" includes business, irrigation, agriculture and system losses. The indoor standard of 47 gallons per person per day comes from California law (AB 1668 / SB 606), effective 2025 (42 by 2030).

        Contamination: station averages of specific conductance (converted to µS/cm) from CEDEN via data.ca.gov; Sweetwater and Loveland reservoirs from USGS via the Water Quality Portal (1998–2009). Stations lie inside a rectangle around the county, so a few border-area stations may appear. CEDEN 2015 and 2016 could not be retrieved. Lake readings below 20 µS/cm were dropped as probable unit errors. "Ocean & tidal" is above 20,000 µS/cm. Station averages mix seasons and sampling programs, so they are a guide, not a ranking of violations.

        Bacteria: enterococcus results (CFU or MPN per 100 mL; qPCR "copies" excluded) for the San Diego Regional Board area, 2020 to Oct 2026, from CEDEN's fecal indicator bacteria dataset on data.ca.gov (includes BeachWatch, MS4 and TMDL monitoring). A single-sample value over 104 per 100 mL is the beach-posting level; the state's own freshwater objectives use E. coli, so inland dots are a screen only. Stations with fewer than 5 samples are omitted. Wet season = Nov–Apr.

        The connection chart joins creek stations to district boundaries with a point-in-polygon test, run in your browser. Many things drive creek salinity besides water use (geology, imported water, tidal influence, urban runoff, sampling programs), so the chart shows a pattern, not a cause.

        Not covered: small water systems, military bases and desert communities; recycled water; and bacteria, nutrients or toxic chemicals. Check your utility and county beach reports for drinking-water and swimming safety. Basemap: Esri World Light Gray (© Esri, HERE, Garmin, OpenStreetMap contributors).
